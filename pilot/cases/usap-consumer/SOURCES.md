# Consumer-focused anesthesia reel — review draft

The video leads with the FTC’s alleged consumer harm, not the source-checking process. No source dates are spoken. Full dates, legal timeline and historical coverage remain in the caption.

- **Extra costs (C9):** FTC complaint ¶8, printed pages 4–5. The FTC alleged anesthesia services from the same anesthesiologists cost Texans tens of millions more each year because of USAP’s roll-up, price-setting and market-allocation strategy. This is an allegation across Texas, not a quantified loss from the Capitol transaction alone. The FTC’s April 2026 investigation/litigation announcement repeats the allegation (S5).
- **Limited patient choice (C10):** complaint ¶46, printed page 19: “patients rarely choose their anesthesia providers.” The complaint explains that a surgeon or operating-room assignment may determine the provider. This is the FTC’s market description, not an invented patient account.
- **Coverage funding (C11):** complaint ¶65–66, printed page 23. Members finance coverage through premiums; the complaint describes employer-funded administrative-services-only plans as well as fully insured coverage. The reel does not claim a quantified premium increase or a verified increase in every patient’s bill.
- **Ownership:** complaint ¶3 describes Welsh Carson creating USAP. Do not imply that every private-equity investment produces the same outcome.
- **Response and legal timeline:** USAP denied the allegations. The caption retains Welsh Carson’s federal dismissal, the separate FTC consent order and the later preliminary agreement. No final liability finding is asserted.
